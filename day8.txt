My observations:

  1. Why WER (ASR) isn't improving:

  "filtering." 
   * The Issue: The errors in the BWC footage are mostly Substitutions. This happens when the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is so low that
     the speech is physically masked by noise (e.g., a siren or wind). 
   * The Limit: Simply making the audio louder (Normalization) or cutting frequencies (Bandpass) doesn't "reveal" the hidden speech; it
     just makes the noise louder or cuts out the high-frequency consonants Whisper needs for clarity.

  2. Why Miss/FA (Diarization) isn't improving:
  These rates are determined by Voice Activity Detection (VAD). 
   * Miss Rate (17-19%): The VAD is ignoring speech that is too quiet or too buried in noise. Normalization helps a little, but if the
     noise floor rises with the speech, the VAD still can't "see" the voice.
   * False Alarm Rate (17-20%): The BWC environment is full of "speech-like" non-speech sounds: rhythmic clothing rustle, static, and
     distant sirens. Our current VAD is mistaking these for human voices.

  Crucial Observations

   1. Normalization (B) is SNR-Neutral:
       * As expected mathematically, Loudness Normalization does not change the SNR. It boosts the signal and the noise floor by the
         exact same amount.
       * Why did DER improve then? Because while the SNR (ratio) stayed the same, the absolute level of the speech became consistent.
         Pyannote's clustering is more sensitive to the absolute spectral profile than it is to the ratio, so making everyone equally
         loud helped it cluster speakers better, even if the noise was also louder.

   2. Bandpass (C) creates a false positive SNR boost:
       * In the Podcast, the SNR improved by +0.83 dB. This is a real improvement—it cut out silence-hiss.
       * In the BWC, it only improved by +0.03 dB. This tiny change confirms that the noise is "in-band" (overlaps with human voice
         frequencies). You cannot filter it out without also filtering the voice.

   3. Why "Before" SNR varies between Pipelines:
       * You'll notice the "Before" SNR for BWC is lower in Pipeline C (7.99 dB) than in Pipeline A (9.07 dB).
       * This is because the VAD in Pipeline C was less accurate. It included more "junk" in its signal segments. When the VAD includes
         noise as "signal," it mathematically lowers the calculated SNR.
       * Conclusion: Preprocessing (especially filtering) is actually confusing our VAD, causing it to grab more noise and call it
         speech. This is why Pipeline C had the highest error rates.
