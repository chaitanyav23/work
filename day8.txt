  3. Understanding SA-WER and WDER

  These metrics represent the interaction between transcription and diarization.

  WDER (Word Diarization Error Rate)
   * What it measures: The percentage of correctly transcribed words that were assigned to the wrong speaker.
   * Impact: In Pipeline B, WDER is 0.1033. This means that out of every 100 words Whisper got right, 10 of them were given to the wrong
     person in the transcript.
   * Bottleneck: This is purely a diarization clustering failure. Pipeline B (Normalization) is the best here because it reduced this
     error significantly compared to Baseline.

  SA-WER (Speaker-Attributed WER)
   * What it measures: The total error rate of the conversation timeline.
   * Formula: SA-WER = Base WER + WDER.
   * Interpretation: A word is only "Perfect" if both the text and the speaker are correct.
   * Comparison: Pipeline B has an SA-WER of 41.12%. This is the most accurate reflection of the "end-user experience"—out of all words
     spoken, about 41% are either wrong text or wrong speaker.

My observations:

  1. Why WER (ASR) isn't improving:
  Whisper (the model we are using) is already trained on massive amounts of noisy data. It has its own internal "normalization" and
  "filtering." 
   * The Issue: The errors in the BWC footage are mostly Substitutions. This happens when the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is so low that
     the speech is physically masked by noise (e.g., a siren or wind). 
   * The Limit: Simply making the audio louder (Normalization) or cutting frequencies (Bandpass) doesn't "reveal" the hidden speech; it
     just makes the noise louder or cuts out the high-frequency consonants Whisper needs for clarity.

  2. Why Miss/FA (Diarization) isn't improving:
  These rates are determined by Voice Activity Detection (VAD). 
   * Miss Rate (17-19%): The VAD is ignoring speech that is too quiet or too buried in noise. Normalization helps a little, but if the
     noise floor rises with the speech, the VAD still can't "see" the voice.
   * False Alarm Rate (17-20%): The BWC environment is full of "speech-like" non-speech sounds: rhythmic clothing rustle, static, and
     distant sirens. Our current VAD is mistaking these for human voices.
