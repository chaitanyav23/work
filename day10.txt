  the same Pyannote 3.1 engine and the same speaker count, Phase 6 is much more aggressive, which has shifted the error profile from
  "Hallucinations" to "Deletions."

  2. Why are the numbers so different for Fixed-2?

  The difference comes down to the "Cleaning" logic and the "Muting" trick implemented in the Phase 6 pipeline:

  A. ASR Deletions (32 vs. 71)
   * Phase 5: Used "Master Grounding" segments that were more generous with timing.
   * Phase 6: Introduced a 0.2-second minimum length filter. Because the subject in bwc.wav speaks in very short bursts (e.g., "Yeah",
     "No", "Nope"), the Phase 6 pipeline is physically deleting these short speech segments before the ASR even starts. This is why
     deletions more than doubled.

  B. Diarization Miss (0.11 vs. 0.39)
   * Phase 5: Processed the entire audio or used wider VAD segments.
   * Phase 6: Used the "Muting Trick" based on the aggressive 0.2s VAD. By silencing the audio between VAD segments, we accidentally
     muted the quiet "word tails" and short utterances. The Diarizer now sees total silence where the Ground Truth expects speech,
     resulting in the massive spike in "Miss" errors.

  C. False Alarm Improvement (0.22 vs. 0.05)
   * This is the success story of Phase 6. By being so aggressive, the pipeline successfully eliminated nearly all of the "Hallucinated
     Speech" caused by sirens and wind noise that plagued Phase 5. Phase 6 is much "quieter" and more focused on loud speech.

  D. Speaker Confusion (0.12 vs. 0.24)
   * Strictness of Evaluator: Phase 6 uses a stricter frame-level evaluator. Because the 3rd and 4th speakers (additional officers) are
     forced into the 2-speaker model, the Phase 6 evaluator captures every millisecond of that mismatch as "Confusion," whereas Phase
     5's evaluation was likely more lenient with the multi-speaker arrest scene.

  Summary
  The Phase 6 Fixed-2 pipeline is mechanically cleaner (far fewer False Alarms) but behaviorally too aggressive (higher
  Deletions/Misses). It is effectively "throwing the baby out with the bathwater" by treating short subject responses as noise. 

  Recommendation: To restore Phase 5 performance while keeping the Phase 6 False Alarm benefits, we should lower the VAD cleaning
  threshold from 0.2s to 0.05s.

  2. The "Fixed-2" Pipeline Logic

  Phase 5 Pipeline (Sequential)
   1. Normalization
   2. Load Static VAD: Used the pre-existing "Master Grounding" JSON.
   3. Diarization: Pyannote 3.1 was given the full audio but told to find 2 speakers. It had the freedom to look at the "silence"
      between words to build its speaker clusters.
   4. ASR: WhisperX processed the whole file at once.
   5. Merge: Results were combined at the end based on timestamps.
   * Result: Better "Recall" (less missing speech) because the models had access to the full audio signal and context.

  Phase 6 Pipeline (Grounded Parallel)
   1. Normalization (+ Enhancement)
   2. Generate Dynamic VAD: Pyannote 3.1 VAD runs on the processed signal.
   3. VAD Cleaning (The Filter): New Step. Applies a strict 0.2s minimum length filter and 0.3s gap merge.
   4. The Muting Trick: The audio is multiplied by a mask. All non-speech areas are set to 0.0 (Absolute Silence).
   5. Parallel Execution:
       * Diarization: Runs on the Muted audio. It only sees what the VAD filter allowed.
       * ASR: Only transcribes the Slices defined by the VAD.
   * Result: Significantly lower "False Alarms" (cleaner output) but higher "Deletions" because if the VAD filter (Step 3) missed a
     word, the rest of the pipeline was physically blinded to it.

  ---

  3. Summary of Technical Impact
  The Phase 6 Architecture is more "Hardened" against noise because of the Muting Trick and Parallel Slicing. However, it is currently
  limited by the VAD Cleaning Filter. 

   * Phase 5 succeeded by being "Loose"—allowing the models to see everything and using a human-vetted grounding.
   * Phase 6 struggled by being "Tight"—it introduced an automated filter that was too aggressive for the short, quiet responses of the
     Subject in the BWC footage.

  Conclusion: The high WER and DER in Phase 6 are not failures of the ASR or Diarization models themselves, but rather a side effect of
  the new Pre-Processing Architecture (Muting/Filtering) being slightly too restrictive for this specific dataset.
