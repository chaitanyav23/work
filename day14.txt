Proposal: Domain-Adaptive Conversational Speaker Attribution for Noisy Body-Worn Camera Audio

Problem Statement

Automatic speaker diarization and conversational transcription systems perform poorly on noisy body-worn camera (BWC) audio due to several severe acoustic and conversational challenges.

Unlike clean meeting or broadcast datasets, BWC recordings contain:

- strong environmental noise,
- reverberation,
- distant/off-axis speech,
- rapid conversational turn-taking,
- overlapping speakers,
- fragmented utterances,
- inconsistent signal-to-noise ratios,
- highly dynamic acoustic conditions.

Modern diarization systems such as NeMo MSDD rely heavily on pretrained speaker embedding extractors (e.g., TitaNet) that are generally trained on cleaner and more structured speech corpora. As a result, speaker embedding spaces become unstable under BWC conditions, especially for:

- short-duration windows,
- overlap-heavy regions,
- noisy conversational transitions.

This instability leads to:

- fragmented speaker trajectories,
- speaker switching artifacts,
- poor local manifold continuity,
- degraded speaker separability,
- unstable diarization outputs.

Additionally, conventional diarization pipelines operate almost entirely on acoustic information and ignore:

- conversational semantics,
- dialogue continuity,
- linguistic structure,
- contextual speaker behavior.

However, human conversational understanding inherently integrates both:

- acoustic identity,
- semantic conversational flow.

This motivates the need for a domain-adaptive multimodal conversational attribution framework capable of:

1. improving speaker embedding robustness under BWC acoustics,
2. stabilizing local speaker manifold geometry,
3. reducing diarization fragmentation artifacts,
4. incorporating conversational semantic context for speaker attribution refinement.

---

Existing Dual-Branch Architecture

The current system is designed as a dual-branch conversational speech processing pipeline.

Branch 1 — Acoustic Diarization Branch

Raw Audio
→ DeepFilterNet Enhancement
→ pyannote Voice Activity Detection
→ Oracle Speech RTTM Generation
→ NeMo MSDD Diarization

Purpose

This branch estimates:
“Who spoke when?”

Design Philosophy

The pipeline intentionally separates:

- speech activity detection,
  from:
- speaker attribution.

By generating oracle speech RTTMs from VAD outputs, the system isolates diarization performance from VAD-induced errors, enabling cleaner speaker attribution analysis.

MSDD Processing

NeMo MSDD performs:

- multiscale segmentation,
- TitaNet speaker embedding extraction,
- temporal speaker refinement,
- overlap-aware diarization decoding.

Multiscale windows:

- 1.5s
- 1.2s
- 1.0s
- 0.8s
- 0.5s

are used to balance:

- speaker identity stability,
- temporal speaker transition resolution.

---

Branch 2 — Linguistic ASR Branch

Raw Audio
→ Manual VAD-Guided Slicing
→ WhisperX ASR + Alignment

Purpose

This branch estimates:
“What was spoken?”

WhisperX provides:

- transcripts,
- word-level timestamps,
- alignment information.

The ASR branch is intentionally decoupled from diarization so that:

- transcription quality,
  and:
- speaker attribution quality

can be analyzed independently.

---

Fusion Stage

Outputs from both branches are fused through temporal alignment.

Speaker RTTMs from MSDD are aligned with:

- utterance timestamps,
- word timestamps,
- transcript segments,

to construct a unified conversational timeline.

Final output:
speaker-attributed conversational transcript.

Example:

Time| Speaker| Transcript
10.2s| Speaker A| “Can I see your license?”
12.8s| Speaker B| “Yeah, one second.”

This transforms the system from:
simple diarization,
into:
conversational attribution.

---

Initial Observations and Motivation

Initial representation analysis of multiscale TitaNet embeddings using:

- PCA,
- UMAP,
- local trajectory analysis,
- fragmentation metrics,
- embedding velocity metrics,

revealed several critical limitations.

Observed Problems

1. Embedding Diffusion at Short Windows

Short-context embeddings (0.5s–0.8s) exhibit:

- diffuse manifolds,
- reduced speaker separability,
- unstable geometric clustering.

2. Speaker Fragmentation

Rapid local speaker oscillations and fragmented trajectories appear in:

- overlap-heavy regions,
- noisy conversational segments.

3. Transition Instability

High-transition local windows exhibit:

- abrupt embedding-space jumps,
- unstable speaker continuity,
- fragmented conversational manifolds.

4. Domain Mismatch

Pretrained TitaNet embeddings appear insufficiently adapted to:

- noisy BWC acoustics,
- distant speech,
- conversational overlap.

---

Proposed Research Direction

The proposed research introduces a domain-adaptive conversational attribution framework consisting of two major improvements.

---

Improvement 1 — Domain-Adaptive Speaker Embedding Learning

Objective

Improve the robustness and separability of speaker embeddings under BWC acoustic conditions.

Hypothesis

The primary bottleneck in BWC diarization performance lies in the instability of the embedding space rather than solely in the diarization backend.

Proposed Approach

Fine-tune the TitaNet speaker embedding model using:

- BWC conversational audio,
- speaker-labeled supervision,
- metric-learning objectives.

Candidate Loss Functions

- ArcFace / AAM-Softmax
- Triplet Loss
- Supervised Contrastive Loss

Goal

Learn an embedding space where:

- same-speaker embeddings become compact,
- different-speaker embeddings become well-separated,
  even under:
- noise,
- overlap,
- short conversational windows.

Expected Improvements

- reduced fragmentation,
- improved speaker continuity,
- tighter local manifolds,
- better overlap robustness,
- improved multiscale embedding stability.

---

Improvement 2 — Transcript-Aware Conversational Speaker Refinement

Objective

Refine speaker attribution using conversational semantic context in addition to acoustic evidence.

Motivation

Pure acoustic diarization frequently fails in:

- overlap-heavy regions,
- rapid conversational exchanges,
- noisy transitions.

However, conversational semantics contain strong speaker continuity cues.

Example:
Consecutive semantically coherent utterances are often likely to belong to the same conversational role or speaker.

Proposed Approach

Introduce a post-diarization conversational refinement module using:

- current utterance transcript,
- previous conversational context,
- next utterance context,
- acoustic confidence,
- embedding-space continuity,
- speaker transition priors.

Conceptual Model

P(speaker_t | acoustics_t, transcript_t, conversational_context)

instead of:

P(speaker_t | acoustics_t)

Goal

Suppress:

- false speaker switches,
- fragmentation artifacts,
- acoustically unstable transitions.

Expected Improvements

- smoother conversational continuity,
- reduced speaker oscillation,
- semantically consistent attribution,
- improved conversational coherence.

---

Representation-Level Evaluation Framework

Unlike traditional diarization evaluation pipelines that rely primarily on:

- DER,
- WER,
- cpWER,

this work introduces representation-centric diagnostics including:

- PCA manifold analysis,
- UMAP visualization,
- local conversational trajectory analysis,
- embedding velocity metrics,
- fragmentation scoring,
- transition density analysis,
- manifold compactness evaluation.

This enables direct geometric analysis of:

- speaker representation quality,
- conversational embedding dynamics,
- transition stability,
- domain adaptation effectiveness.

---

Long-Term Vision

The long-term goal is to evolve the system from:
acoustic-only diarization,
into:
multimodal conversational representation learning.

Future directions include:

- joint acoustic-semantic embedding spaces,
- conversational transformer-based speaker refinement,
- multimodal speaker attribution,
- graph-based conversational manifold modeling,
- semantic-aware diarization systems.

Ultimately, the system aims to model not only:
“Who spoke when?”
but:
“Who said what, in what conversational context, under noisy real-world acoustic conditions?”
